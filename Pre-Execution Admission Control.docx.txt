Pre-Execution Admission Control(PEAC): A Unified Strategy for Mitigating Resource-Exhaustion Attacks in Managed Blockchain Runtimes


Abstract
Managed runtimes like the JVM and .NET CLR are increasingly used in blockchain clients for their safety and developer productivity. However, they introduce a novel vulnerability: the Garbage Collection (GC) Conflict. Malicious state-expansion attacks (dust) and "execution bombs" trigger non-deterministic Stop-The-World (STW) pauses, leading to validator jitter or ingestion collapse. We propose Pre-Execution Admission Control, a local defense layer that filters transactions using static features strongly correlated with high-allocation behavior before they enter the managed heap. Our cross-runtime evaluation using Hyperledger Besu (JVM) and Nethermind (.NET CLR) demonstrates that this strategy reduces P99 latency in Besu by 48.5% and prevents the 6.7% ingestion failure rate observed in vanilla Nethermind configurations under stress. This work proves that proactive admission is a critical, consensus-neutral requirement for node resilience in adversarial environments.
________________


1. Introduction
The security of decentralized networks depends on the deterministic performance of individual nodes. While Ethereum’s gas model prices execution steps, it fails to account for the allocation-to-GC overhead—the CPU cycles and memory management pauses required to clean ephemeral objects created during execution.
In managed environments, high allocation rates lead to "Micro-Stuttering," where tail-latency spikes (P99) cause validators to miss block proposals. We argue that current defenses, such as database pruning, are reactive and occur too late in the pipeline to prevent VM-level exhaustion. This paper introduces a unified, local admission policy that identifies "toxic" transactions at the node’s perimeter, shielding the execution engine from allocation-driven Denial-of-Service (DoS).
________________


2. Background and Threat Model
2.1 Managed Runtime Vulnerabilities
* JVM (Besu): Uses G1GC, which suffers from marking overhead and long pauses when the "Old Generation" fills with state-bloat objects.
* .NET CLR (Nethermind): Vulnerable to Large Object Heap (LOH) fragmentation and expensive Generation 2 collections when processing transactions with large calldata or access lists.
2.2 Threat Model
* Attacker Profile: An adversary floods the node via public JSON-RPC or the P2P gossip network.
* Attack Vectors: * Execution Bombs: Transactions exhibiting static features (e.g., extreme calldata length) correlated with massive heap allocations.
   * Dust Attacks: Low-value transactions designed to slowly expand the world state, increasing future GC marking times.
* Non-Consensus Constraint: The system operates strictly as a local admission policy; it does not change block validity or network consensus.

Whitelisting and safety constraints
To avoid false positives for protocol-critical components, PEAC supports an operator-configured whitelist of contract addresses(e.g., governance contracts, oracle feeds, and rollup bridges). Transactions targeting whitelisted addresses bypass the economic sanity signal but remain subject to the static allocation risk filter. This ensures that operational safety is preserved while maintaining protection against allocation-driven denial-of-service patterns.

Feature rationale
calldata_len.
Large calldata directly increases decoding overhead, transient buffer allocation, and parameter materialization costs in both clients. Profiling shows a strong positive correlation between calldata length and short-lived allocation volume in the execution and ABI decoding paths.
gas_limit.
While gas is a protocol-level execution bound, it is also a practical upper bound on the size and complexity of the execution trace. Higher gas limits correlate with deeper execution paths and larger transient object graphs in managed runtimes.
access_list_size.
Large access lists induce additional state prefetching, caching, and temporary state view construction, increasing allocation pressure in state access and caching subsystems.
is_contract_creation.
Contract creation triggers bytecode deployment, constructor execution, and metadata generation, which collectively exhibit a significantly higher allocation footprint than ordinary message calls.
Motivation for a multi-dimensional filter
A single-dimensional threshold(e.g., calldata length or gas limit alone) is insufficient to capture allocation-driven attack patterns. Adversaries can easily evade such defenses by distributing pressure across multiple transaction fields. 
By combining multiple static indicators, PEAC approximates the allocation risk surface observed in real execution traces while preserving consensus neutrality and avoiding any form of speculative execution.
Weight calibration
The feature weights  are derived through offline profiling runs in which per-transaction heap allocation volume and GC time are recorded under controlled workloads.
For both clients, weights are fitted using linear regression against observed allocated bytes per transaction. We intentionally restrict the model to a linear form to minimize runtime overhead and to ensure transparent, operator-auditable behavior.

4. Evaluation
4.1 Methodology
Tests were conducted with a workload of 5,000 transactions at a steady 10 tx/sec to ensure identical pressure across configurations.
4.2 Besu Results (Stability)
* Latency Win: P99 pause time dropped from 58.07 ms to 29.89 ms.
* Causal Link: "Time in GC" decreased from 0.18% to 0.05%, proving that the latency reduction is a direct result of lower heap pressure.
4.3 Nethermind Results (Availability)
* Availability Win: Prevented the 6.7% queue overflow failure rate (337 out of 5,000 txs) seen in vanilla runs.
* Memory Integrity: dotnet-trace confirmed the complete elimination of expensive Generation 2 and LOH collections during the attack.
________________


5. Discussion
* Admission Overhead: The CPU cost of pre-execution filtering is negligible compared to the "Stop-The-World" penalties of post-execution management.
* MEV and Proposer Duties: We acknowledge that local rejection may reduce a proposer's MEV candidate pool. We recommend that operators selectively toggle filtering during high-value block construction phases.
* Limitations: Our approach relies on coarse-grained static indicators and cannot prevent all forms of resource abuse that require dynamic execution paths to detect.
